# THE WOLF OF McKENZIE TOWNE
## A Partnership & Funding Proposal to the McKenzie Towne Council
### *Prepared for Keith Dsouza, President, and the Board of Directors*

---

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

**The opportunity.** *The Wolf of McKenzie Towne* is a youth-led, community-wide event planned for **late August 2027**. Teams of local teens will race through McKenzie Towne's themed parks, the High Street and Elgin Hill in an Amazing Race-style adventure. It ends with a dusk finale at which the community relights a symbolic "Lantern" and residents switch on their porch lights together. The event is designed and run by a council of 10–12 teens, supported by screened adult volunteers and post-secondary student mentors.

**Why it matters to the McKenzie Towne Council.** McKenzie Towne was planned as a village, with porches, squares, a High Street and themed parks. This event puts that design to work. It brings residents into Council-maintained spaces, engages teenagers (the age group community organizations most often miss), and gives the Towne a new annual tradition with the Council's name on it.

**What we are asking.** We request that the McKenzie Towne Council (“the Council”) become the event's **Founding Partner**:

| Component | Amount | How it is used |
|---|---|---|
| **Core contribution** | **$5,000** | Released on approval. Funds planning, safeguarding, insurance and the matching share required by City grants. |
| **Match-contingent fund** | **up to $10,000** | Held by the Council. Released **only dollar-for-dollar against confirmed grant and sponsor funding**, in two tranches (February and May 2027). |
| **In-kind support** | est. **$3,800** | Use of McKenzie Towne Hall for planning sessions, a build space and race-day headquarters; Prestwick Common as a station; the Council's communication channels; and prop storage for Season Two. |
| **Total commitment** | **up to $15,000 cash + in-kind** | |

**The safeguard.** The Council's money is protected by design. Two-thirds of the cash is released only when outside funding is secured, every dollar is reported, and **unspent funds are returned**.

**What the Council receives.** Presenting-partner billing (*"The Wolf of McKenzie Towne, presented by the McKenzie Towne Council"*), naming of the finale (*"The McKenzie Towne Council Lantern Lighting"*), a speaking role at the finale, visibility on every channel and printed piece, a youth presentation to the Board, a full impact report, and first right to partner on Season Two.

**The decision requested.** Approval of the Founding Partner commitment, by the motion set out in Part 10.

## PART 1 · THE EVENT AT A GLANCE

| | |
|---|---|
| **What** | An Amazing Race-style adventure for teens aged 13–18, in teams ("clans") of four |
| **When** | A Saturday evening in late August 2027 (date to be confirmed), about 5:30–9:30 p.m. |
| **Where** | McKenzie Towne Hall, High Street, Firetruck Park, Pirate & Castle Park, Inverness Square, Elgin Ship and Dragon playgrounds, Promenade Park and Elgin Hill |
| **Who** | 50–70 teen racers; a Youth Race Council of 10–12 teen leaders; 30+ adult volunteers; post-secondary student mentors; families and neighbours as audience |
| **Story** | A light-hearted legend drawn from the Towne's Scottish street names. A "Wolf" has stolen the Lantern of the North, and the teens must recover it by dusk. |
| **Finale** | The Lantern is relit at dusk, and residents across the Towne switch on their porch lights |
| **Also** | An afternoon "Cubs' Quest" for younger children at the themed playgrounds |

The event is youth-led by design. The Youth Race Council, which includes the President and Vice-President of a local 4-H club, works through a structured twelve-step planning program over the coming months. Adults hold responsibility for safety, finances, permits and approvals.

## PART 2 · WHY THIS FITS THE COUNCIL'S MANDATE

The McKenzie Towne Council owns, maintains and manages McKenzie Towne's shared amenities, and enriches resident life through programming and community events. This proposal supports that mandate in five ways:

1. **It shows off the Council's amenities.** The event is built around Council-maintained spaces and the Towne's distinctive design. Residents and visitors will see the parks, the Hall and the High Street at their best.
2. **It reaches teens.** The Council already serves young children and families well. Teenagers are harder to reach, and this event gives them a reason to engage with the community and its spaces.
3. **It builds the Council's future volunteers.** Teens who lead this event become the next generation of Council volunteers, program leaders and engaged residents.
4. **It strengthens neighbour relationships.** Porch-cheer streets, the community finale and the porch-light moment turn a resident base into a neighbourhood.
5. **It complements, rather than competes with, the Council's event calendar.** It fills a late-summer gap alongside the Council's established events, such as Music in the Park, Halloween Spooktacular and the Christmas Craft Fair, and can become an annual tradition.

**How we will protect the Council's spaces.** The themed playgrounds are built for young children, so teens will use them as backdrops and complete tasks beside the equipment, not on it. Stormwater ponds are strictly off-limits. Every park is left cleaner than we found it.

## PART 3 · GOVERNANCE AND SAFEGUARDING

**Structure**
- **Youth Race Council (10–12 teens):** designs the game, story and publicity; runs stations on race day.
- **Adult Steering Group:** Event Lead, Safety Officer, Finance Lead and Volunteer Lead. Holds all legal, financial and safety responsibility.
- **Council Liaison (requested):** one Council director or staff member as observer on the Steering Group, with full visibility and minimal time (about one meeting a month).
- **Student mentors:** post-secondary marketing and theatre students on supervised placements.

**Safeguarding commitments**
- Police information checks with vulnerable-sector verification for every adult volunteer and student mentor
- The two-adult rule at all times, and group-only communication with youth
- Parental consent, waivers, medical and allergy forms, and photo consent for every participant
- The race-day "chase" game confined to a coned, lit, marshalled zone on Elgin Hill, using flag belts only
- Walk-only zones on every street and crossing; no access to stormwater ponds
- Certified first aid on site, and a weather plan that moves activities into McKenzie Towne Hall

**Insurance.** Special-event liability coverage will be arranged, either through a dedicated event policy or by confirming coverage with the Council's insurer, with the McKenzie Towne Council named as an additional insured. **No activity proceeds without confirmed coverage.**

**Conflict of interest.** Any Council director involved in organizing this event will declare the interest and abstain from the Council's discussion and vote on this proposal.

## PART 5 · FUNDING PLAN

**Our strategy is to apply to every eligible source, and to ask each one for its maximum.** We know many funders will award less than we ask, and some won't fund us at all. Applying broadly and asking boldly is how a first-year community event reaches its Target budget. The full research is in the companion *Funding Opportunities Register* (26 sources reviewed: federal, provincial, municipal, foundation and corporate).

**What we realistically expect:** we weight each ask by its chance of success and by the share funders typically award. On that basis, Season One outside funding is expected at **about $50,000**, close to the **$49,730** Target budget. That is an average, not a promise. Some applications will exceed it and others will come up empty. **The McKenzie Towne Council's partnership is what turns that uncertainty into a guaranteed event.**

### Why the Council's contribution is pivotal
1. **It provides the matching funds that larger grants require.** The City's Inspiring Neighbourhoods Grant requires an equal match. The Alberta CIP grant requires the applicant's contribution to at least equal the request, and caps provincial funding at 50% of project costs. The Council's cash (together with volunteer hours, which CIP values at $20–$40 an hour) is what makes these large asks eligible.
2. **It guarantees the event.** If grants fall short, the Council's commitment ensures the event proceeds at the Lean level rather than being cancelled.
3. **It signals confidence.** A strong commitment from the community's own amenities organization makes every other application more competitive.
4. **It costs the Council less as other funding succeeds.** Two-thirds of the Council's cash is released only against confirmed outside funding. If grants come in strongly, those funds are simply not drawn.

### Season Two pipeline (2028)
A successful first year opens much larger sources, several of which need a track record:
- **Building Communities through Arts & Heritage: Local Festivals (Canadian Heritage):** up to **$200,000**, for festivals with a previous edition (deadline 15 Oct 2027)
- **Canada Post Community Foundation:** up to **$25,000** for youth programming (Feb 2027 application, Sept 2027 decision)
- **New Horizons for Seniors:** up to **$50,000** for an intergenerational strand, with seniors as storytellers and porch captains
- **Charity-partner sources** (TELUS Friendly Future Foundation, Calgary Foundation Community Grants, Calgary Flames Foundation): up to **$20,000–$100,000** each

With Season One delivered, the Council's share of future seasons should fall substantially.

## PART 8 · ACCOUNTABILITY AND FINANCIAL CONTROLS

- **A dedicated account:** all event funds are held in a dedicated account of the host organization, with **two signatures** required on every payment.
- **Budget tracking:** expenses are tracked against the approved budget. Variances over 10% in any category are reported to the Council Liaison.
- **Monthly updates:** a one-page status report to the Council covering progress, funding secured, spending and risks.
- **Final report:** a full financial and impact report within **90 days** of the event.
- **Return of funds:** any unspent Council contribution is **returned to the McKenzie Towne Council** or, with the Council's written agreement, held for Season Two.
- **Records:** receipts and records are available to the Council's Controller on request.

## PART 9 · RISKS AND MITIGATION

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| **Grant funding falls short** | Match-contingent release protects the Council; the event scales to the Lean budget rather than overspending |
| **Participant injury** | A safeguarding plan, a marshalled chase zone, walk-only streets, first aid on site, and insurance before activity |
| **Damage to amenities** | Play structures used as backdrops only; a park-care crew; cleanup commitment; a Council walk-through before and after |
| **Weather** | An indoor backup in McKenzie Towne Hall; a lightning or hail protocol |
| **Volunteer shortfall** | Student mentors, summer staff and street-by-street recruitment; clear, short volunteer shifts |
| **Reputational risk** | Vetted adults, a two-adult rule, a Council liaison, and transparent reporting |
| **Noise or neighbour concerns** | Advance notice to nearby residents; amplified sound ends well before quiet hours |
