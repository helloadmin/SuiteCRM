## ABOUT THE PRODUCTION

**The stage is a neighbourhood. The cast is a whole community. The curtain rises at dusk.**

*The Wolf of McKenzie Towne* is a **community-wide theatre production** in the form of an Amazing Race. On one summer evening in **late August 2027**, clans of teens will race through the parks of McKenzie Towne, a village-style community in southeast Calgary, to recover the stolen *Lantern of the North* from a villain called **the Wolf**.

Every park is a set. Every challenge is a scene: a bank heist at Firetruck Park, a pirate-or-castle choice at the Pirate & Castle Park, a power ballad for a wigged tribunal in the gazebo, a pool-noodle joust with a dragon, and a chase up Elgin Hill at dusk to the castle ruins. It ends with a finale where the Lantern is relit and **the entire neighbourhood switches on its porch lights**.

**The production at a glance**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Cast** | 50–70 teen racers, a Youth Race Council of 10–12 teen leaders, costumed characters (a 347-year-old Laird, the Wolf, a dragon, a tribunal) |
| **Crew** | 30+ adult volunteers, merchants, families and student mentors |
| **Audience** | The whole community, from porches, lawn chairs and the finale square |
| **Venue** | Eight themed parks, a High Street and a hilltop "ruin" |
| **Run** | One night only (plus a family matinee for younger kids) |

It's **youth-led and adult-supported**. A Youth Race Council of teens, including 4-H officers, designs and runs the show through a twelve-mission planning season. **That's where you come in.** We're looking for post-secondary students who bring **hard work, vision, creativity and fun** to coach those young leaders and help bring the production to life.

## POSTING 1 · THEATRE MENTOR: DIRECTION & PERFORMANCE
### *Practicum / community service-learning placement*

**Role in the production:** Assistant Director and Character Coach

| | |
|---|---|
| **Placement type** | Practicum, internship or community service-learning (for credit) |
| **Programs** | Drama, Theatre Arts, Performance, Applied Theatre, Theatre for Young Audiences |
| **Term** | Winter term (January–April 2027), with an option to continue through race day in late August |
| **Hours** | About 3–5 hours a week, including one weekly evening planning session (about 90 minutes) |
| **Compensation** | Course credit, plus a **$750 season honorarium** (subject to funding) |
| **Positions** | 1 |

**What you'll do**
- **Bring the characters to life.** Lead the character-creation and casting workshops (Mission 3, "The Legend"). Coach the teens playing the Laird, the Wolf, Dodgy Dougal, the Dragon and the Gazebo Tribunal.
- **Shape the script.** Help the Story Crew write the opening, the Wolf's taunts, the QR-code video scripts and the finale.
- **Direct the videos.** Direct the Laird and Wolf videos that play at each station (Mission 9).
- **Stage the trials.** Help the Station Captains turn each challenge into a short, playable scene.
- **Run the dress rehearsal like a tech run.** Pacing, cues, transitions and the finale countdown (Mission 10).
- **Coach presence.** Teach stage presence and voice to teens pitching sponsors and presenting to community boards.
- **On race day:** support the performers and help call the finale.

**What you'll gain**
- A credited directing and devising role in a large-scale, site-specific community production
- Experience in applied theatre and theatre for young audiences, working with youth ensembles
- Portfolio material: production photos, video, scripts and a highlight reel
- A reference letter from the production leads

**You might be a fit if you…**
- are studying drama, theatre or a related field and love ensemble and devised work
- can direct with warmth, energy and a sense of humour
- are reliable, organized, and comfortable coaching teens aged 13–18
- believe theatre belongs in parks as much as on stages

## WHAT EVERY ROLE SHARES

**The four things we're looking for**
- **Hard work.** This is a real production with real deadlines and real teens counting on you.
- **Vision.** Help the youth see what their community could become in one night.
- **Creativity.** Invent, adapt and solve problems on a community budget.
- **Fun.** If you aren't having fun, neither is the cast. Bring the joy.

**How mentors work with youth**
- **The youth lead, and you sharpen them.** You coach, ask questions and teach skills. You don't take over.
- **Weekly rhythm:** one evening planning session per week (following the Youth Council's twelve-mission planning guide), plus flexible project time.
- **Supervision:** you'll have a named on-site supervisor and adult allies at every session. We'll complete any learning plans and evaluations your program requires.

**Safeguarding requirements (all roles)**
- A **police information check with vulnerable-sector verification** (we'll guide you through it)
- Following the **two-adult rule**: never alone with a youth
- Communicating with youth only in **group channels that include an adult ally**
- A short orientation on youth safeguarding, the Council Charter and reporting duties

**Inclusion**
We welcome applicants of every background, ability and experience. If you need accommodations to apply or take part, tell us and we'll work with you.
